Trade face down. Lie if you dare. Corner the market.
Rulebook2 to 6 players15 to 30 minutesVersion 0.19.0

1The idea

Everyone is trying to corner one market: to hold five of the same resource and keep them for a full round. Nobody can see anybody else's cards, every trade is made face down, and anything you say about a card may be a lie. Catch a liar and you profit. Accuse an honest player and you pay for it.

The first player to score the agreed number of Monopoly tokens wins: 1 for a quick game, 2 for a normal one, 3 for a long one.

5Trading

There is no free way to swap cards. You change your row by trading, by paying for cards, and by winning calls.

You
It's Oil. I want Farmland.
⇄
Them
Here's your Farmland.
  1. Pick a player. Put one resource face down in the middle, say what it is (you may lie) and say what you want back.
  2. They decline (your card goes back), or trade (they put a card down and say what it is, and they may lie too), or call scam on your card, putting one of their own cards down as a stake.
  3. If they traded, you swap the two cards unseen, or call scam on theirs.
The card was a lieThe caller keeps both cards.
The card was honestThe accused keeps both cards, and the caller takes the wrong-call penalty.

Calling it off

If they say they are giving you something other than what you asked for, you may call the trade off instead. Both cards go back and nobody is penalised.

Gaps

A card in a trade leaves its place empty. If it comes back, it goes back into that place.

Open offers

Instead of naming a player you may offer your card to the table. It goes clockwise from your left; each player may trade, call scam or pass, and the first to answer gets the trade. If everyone passes the card returns to you. Either way it was your one trade.

Paid: Farmland.

Most cards cost a particular resource, shown at the top of the card. You pay face down and say you paid the right thing. If you do not have it, you can pay with something else and bluff.

Going clockwise from your left, each other player says pass or call. The first to call makes the call, and the payment is turned over.

Caught lyingYour card is wasted and the caller takes your payment.
Called, but honestYour card works and the caller takes the wrong-call penalty.
Nobody callsYour card works. The payment is discarded unseen.

Free cards need no payment, so nobody can call them.

7Heat and Cover

Heat and Cover are two ends of one scale. Nobody ever has both.

You called and were rightRemove 1 Heat. With no Heat, gain 1 Cover (at most 3).
You called and were wrongSpend 1 Cover. With no Cover, gain 1 Heat (at most 5).

The Whistleblower

Anyone may play it at any moment, even in the middle of someone else's trade. Name a player.

Right: they have five of a kind right now. They show you their whole row and you take any two cards for yourself.

Wrong: you take the wrong-call penalty. Then the game carries on from where it stopped.

Your turn at a glance

1

Start

Score a held monopoly and use its power · refill your row · draw 1 card

2

Main

One trade · any number of cards

3

End

Discard to your limits · place a Hold marker

Any time, anyone: Whistleblower

Calls

Right: lose 1 Heat, else gain 1 Cover.

Wrong: lose 1 Cover, else gain 1 Heat. At 5 Heat, lose your leftmost card.

Each Heat turns one of your cards face up, from the left.

Remember

Never rearrange your row. New cards join on the right.

You can only lie about what a card is, never about the rules.

Five of a kind, kept for a full round, scores.

At a real table

There are no timers. Calls on a payment go clockwise from the payer's left.

Slide a traded card forward out of the row instead of closing the row up.

A boycotted player keeps a Boycott marker until their next turn ends; Boosts, the Whistleblower and the Alibi are still allowed.

On screen

The host can choose whether cards you have seen keep a small symbol: all of them, only ones you looked at yourself, or none.

Bots come in four difficulties. The harder ones read how you play as well as what you show.
