Handland

Trade face down. Lie if you dare. Corner the market.

2 to 6 players · about 15 minutes · card game rules

Goal

Be the first to earn the agreed number of Monopoly tokens: 1 for a short game, 2 for a standard game, 3 for a long one. You earn a token by holding five of one resource for a full round without anyone breaking it up.

Setup

  1. Shuffle the resource deck and the Action/Boost deck separately. Each has its own discard pile.
  2. Deal each player 5 resource cards, face down in a row in front of them. These are your slots. You may look at your own at any time. Nobody else may.
  3. Deal each player a hand of 3 Action/Boost cards.
  4. Give each player their two Hold markers. Everyone starts with no Heat and no Cover.
  5. Pick a first player at random. Play goes clockwise.

Never rearrange your row. Cards keep their place, and new cards always join at the right end. (Heat turns cards face up from the left.)

Your turn

1 · Start

  • Flip your Hold marker. If it says HOLDING and you still have five of a kind, score (see Monopolies).
  • Refill every empty slot from the resource deck. Draw 1 Action/Boost card.

2 · Main, in any order

  • Offer one trade.
  • Play as many hand cards as you like.

3 · End

  • Discard down to your slot limit (5, plus Warehouses) and to 3 hand cards.
  • Place a Hold marker face down: HOLDING if you have five of a kind right now, otherwise NOT HOLDING.

There is no free way to swap resources. You change what you hold by trading, by paying for cards (the slot refills next turn) and by winning call-outs.

Trading

  1. Pick a player. Put one resource face down in the middle, say what it is (you may lie) and say what you want back.
  2. They choose one:
    • Decline. Your card comes back.
    • Trade. They put one of their resources face down and say what it is (they may lie too). Go to step 3.
    • Call scam. They put one of their resources down as a stake and call your card. Flip yours.
  3. If they traded, you choose: accept and swap the two cards unseen, or call scam on their card and flip it. If they said something other than what you asked for, you may instead call it off: both cards go back.
When a card is flipped by a call:
It was a lie: the caller keeps both cards on the table.
It was honest: the accused keeps both cards, and the caller takes the call-out penalty.
A card put into a trade leaves a gap in your row. If it comes back, it goes back into that gap.

Open offers

Instead of naming a player, offer your card to the table. The offer goes clockwise, starting on your left. Each player in turn may trade, call scam, or pass. The first player who answers gets the trade. If everyone passes, the card comes back. Either way, that was your one trade.

Paying for cards

Most cards have a cost. Put that many resources face down and say they are what the card asks for. You may pay with the wrong cards: that is a bluff.

Starting on your left and going clockwise, each other player says pass or call. The first to call makes the call; flip the payment.

  • Caught lying: your card is wasted (discard it) and the caller takes the payment cards.
  • Called but honest: your card works, the payment is discarded, and the caller takes the call-out penalty.
  • Everyone passes: your card works. The payment goes to the discard pile face down. Nobody may look.

Small print

  • Empty deck: shuffle its discard pile.
  • Calling a trade offer needs a stake: put one of your own resources down before the flip.
  • Too many resources: keep them until the end of your own turn, then discard down.
  • Alibi on a trade call ends that trade (cards go back). On a payment call, the payment counts as let through.
  • Injunction delays scoring one turn. If the holder still has five a round later, it scores then. Nobody is told whether anything was stopped.
  • Winning: the game ends the moment someone takes their last needed token.

Heat and Cover

Heat and Cover are two ends of one scale. Nobody ever has both.

You called and were right
Remove 1 Heat. If you have none, gain 1 Cover (most 3).
You called and were wrong
Spend 1 Cover. If you have none, gain 1 Heat (most 5). Already at 5: your leftmost card is destroyed.

Heat exposes you. For each Heat you have, one of your resources lies face up, starting from the left of your row. New cards always join at the right. If a face-up card leaves, turn the next one over.

Cover does nothing by itself. It soaks up your next wrong calls.

Monopolies and the hold

  • At the end of your turn you place a Hold marker face down. HOLDING means you have five of one resource right now. A Duplicator makes its card count as 2.
  • If you still have five of that resource when your next turn starts, flip the marker, show your resources, discard the five (the fewest cards that add up to five), take a Monopoly token and refill your slots.
  • If you dropped below five at any moment in between, the hold is broken. Say nothing. On your turn, flip the marker and carry on; there is no penalty.
  • You must place HOLDING when you have five of a kind, and NOT HOLDING when you do not.
Whistleblower. Anyone may play it at any moment, even in the middle of someone else's trade. Name a player.
Right (they can show five of a kind right now, whatever their marker says): they show you all their resources, and you take any two for yourself.
Wrong (anything else): you take the wrong-call penalty.
Then the game carries on from where it stopped.

Monopoly powers

When you score, you may use that resource's power once, right then. It is free, cannot be called, and you may skip it. The winning token has no power.

Turn summary

Start: flip your marker and score if you held · refill slots · draw 1.
Main: one trade · any number of cards.
End: discard to limits · place a Hold marker.
Any time: Whistleblower.
